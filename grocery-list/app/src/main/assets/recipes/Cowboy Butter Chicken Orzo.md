# Cowboy Butter Chicken Orzo

**Source:** [Facebook reel](https://www.facebook.com/reel/2013991062593152/) (shared via https://www.facebook.com/share/r/1WY8zMtWtt/)
**Creator:** Mamas Down South Kitchen
**Servings:** 4–6 *(estimate)*
**Prep time:** 10 min *(estimate)*
**Cook time:** 25–30 min *(estimate)*
**Pan:** a large covered electric skillet

> She says the full recipe, a shopping list, and cookbook info are under the pinned Facebook comment, and that this one is also in her new cookbook. The reel itself gives almost no amounts. Amounts below are estimates unless they’re marked as shown or taken from what she says.

## Ingredients
- 1½–2 lb chicken, diced raw (it looks like breast) *(amount and the cut are estimates)*
- Oil, for the pan *(amount not shown; estimate: 1–2 tbsp)*
- Kinder’s Cowboy Butter seasoning, for the chicken and later for the broth *(amount not shown; estimate: about 1 tsp on the chicken, plus about 1 tsp into the broth)*
- Great Value paprika, for the chicken and later for the broth *(amount not shown; estimate: about 1 tsp on the chicken, plus about 1 tsp into the broth)*
- Garlic powder, for the chicken *(amount not shown; estimate: about 1 tsp)*
- Black pepper, for the chicken *(amount not shown; estimate: about ½ tsp, or to taste)*
- Kinder’s All Purpose seasoning, for the chicken *(amount not shown; estimate: about 1 tsp)*
- 2–3 tbsp butter *(estimate; she says “a few tablespoons,” and a pat of butter is shown)*
- 2 cups dry orzo *(shown: two level scoops with a 1-cup measure)*
- 3–4 cups chicken broth, poured from a quart mason jar *(amount is an estimate; the jar is shown)*
- Great Value Dijon mustard *(amount not shown; estimate: 1–2 tbsp)*
- Lemon juice, from a ReaLemon-style bottle *(amount not shown; she says “a little bit”; estimate: 1–2 tsp)*
- ½–1 cup Great Value heavy whipping cream *(estimate)*
- About ¾ cup Great Value finely shredded Parmesan *(estimate; it looks like about half of a 1½-cup bag)*
- Dried parsley, for garnish *(shown)*
- Garlic knots, for serving *(she says not to forget them)*

## Steps
1. Heat the oil in the electric skillet. Add the diced raw chicken and season it in the pan with the Cowboy Butter seasoning, paprika, garlic powder, black pepper, and All Purpose seasoning. Cook just until you get some color on it; she says it doesn’t have to be done yet. The chicken stays in the pan the whole time.
2. Add the butter and let it melt. Add the orzo and toast it for a few minutes if you want; she likes to toast it, but she says that’s optional.
3. Pour in the chicken broth. Stir in the Dijon, a little lemon juice, and more Cowboy Butter seasoning and paprika *(about 1 tsp each is an estimate; she adds full measuring spoons of what look like those two into the broth)*.
4. Let it come to a simmer, then cover and cook until the liquid is absorbed and the orzo is tender, about 12–15 min *(time is an estimate; she just says to cook until the liquid is absorbed and the orzo is tender)*.
5. Stir in the heavy cream and Parmesan until the cheese melts.
6. Finish with dried parsley and serve with garlic knots.

## Tips
- Her official amounts are in the pinned comment on the Facebook reel, and she says this recipe is also in her new cookbook, which was on pre-sale when she posted. Use those if you can get them; everything measured here is only an estimate from the voiceover and the video, except the 2 cups of orzo, which is clearly shown.
- Kinder’s Cowboy Butter and Kinder’s All Purpose are the two named seasoning blends she uses. If you can’t find the Cowboy Butter blend, don’t swap in a random “ taco” mix; look for a buttery, garlicky, slightly spicy blend, or use the pinned comment if it lists a substitute *(that swap is a suggestion, not from the reel)*.
- The chicken is diced before it goes in the pan and is not taken out to cube later.
- Toast the orzo if you want a nuttier flavor; she likes it, but she says you can skip that step.
- A quart mason jar of broth is shown, so start with about 3 cups and add more if the orzo is still firm before the liquid is gone *(that’s an estimate)*.
